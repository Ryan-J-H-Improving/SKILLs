# Learning Pacing Protocol

## Purpose

Keep first-exposure teaching coherent without multiplying checks, repeatedly asking permission to proceed, or advancing from shallow evidence.

## Teaching Unit

A knowledge point is one coherent relationship, method, comparison, decision rule, or concept family. Supporting vocabulary and prerequisite repair do not automatically become additional points or exercises.

Use the validated blueprint as the canonical order. Teach one point, issue its one exercise set, mark it, and then either advance automatically or repair the exact blocker.

## Narrative Continuity

Every point must begin by answering:

1. What prior idea it depends on.
2. Why the course introduces it now.
3. What problem, limitation, or question it resolves.
4. Which terms are genuinely new.

Recover this from adjacent slides and the matching transcript passage. If both sources jump, add only a minimal labeled `AI-added bridge`. Do not begin with an unexplained table, formula, plot, or term list.

## One-Set Practice Economy

First-exposure learning has one exercise set per point, not a readiness set followed by an independent set.

- Default to three answer actions.
- Increase to four or five only for distinct examinable operations.
- Do not generate separate exercises for supporting terms unless they are genuine course targets.
- Do not repeat the worked example or ask multiple near-identical recognition questions.
- Reuse valid spontaneous explanations when they directly satisfy a planned action, and omit only that covered action.
- Keep spaced mixed review separate from the current point.

## Automatic Advancement

After a valid answer with no unresolved conceptual problem:

1. Give concise diagnostic feedback.
2. Update point status and numeric progress.
3. Begin the next blueprint point in the same response.

Do not ask whether the user wants to continue. Ask a choice only when the next step requires a consequential decision that cannot be inferred, such as conflicting course instances or an explicit review-style choice.

`Continue`, `okay`, `no problem`, `I see`, and silence are pacing signals, not understanding evidence. If the user skips the exercise, the tutor may proceed but must preserve the point as taught without valid practice evidence.

## Remediation Budget

For one valid conceptual error, use one concise repair and one different targeted item. If the user remains wrong, ask for their mental model before a second explanation. Do not surround one misunderstanding with a new multi-question set.

For a tutor coverage gap, repair the blueprint and state before asking a replacement item. A repair does not advance mastery.

## Load And Fatigue

Stop immediately when the user says they are tired, angry, wants to pause, does not want more questions, or wants to end. Do not append a quiz, continuation question, or extensive recap.

Treat repeated short answers, complaints about question volume, repeated reports of sudden content, or repeated tutor repairs as load signals. Shorten the next explanation, preserve the last stable point, and audit the remaining blueprint.

Two tutor coverage failures in the same lesson require a complete remaining-lesson audit before resumption. Do not continue local patching indefinitely.

## Durable Update Economy

Persist state after a point completes, a misconception is genuinely corrected, the blueprint changes, a tutor record is repaired, or the session stops. Do not update and narrate multiple files after every micro-clarification. The current-state block is canonical; append-only logs provide evidence without replacing it.
